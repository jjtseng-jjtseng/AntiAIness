What Pulse records

Per endpoint, not averaged across the API.

A single number for the whole service hides the route that's hurting. Pulse keeps latency, errors and throughput for each endpoint separately, so a slower GET /v1/orders doesn't vanish behind a quick GET /v1/search.

Latency p50 · p95 · p99
The typical request and the slow tail, side by side, for every endpoint.
Error rate per endpoint
See which route is failing instead of one blended rate for the whole API.
Throughput req/min
Traffic next to latency, so a load spike and a regression don't look the same.
Retention up to 13 months
7 days on Free, 30 days on Team, 13 months on Business: enough to compare with the same month last year.

Incident timeline

The incident and the deploy behind it, on one axis.

Deploy markers sit on the same timeline as your latency and error data. When 5xx responses start climbing, the deploy that lines up with them is right there, not in another tab.

Sample incident. The time window, endpoint and deploy are sample data; the shape of the 5xx line is illustrative.

Alerts

A threshold for each endpoint, not one for the whole API.

Checkout and search don't share a normal. Give each endpoint its own threshold, and send alerts to Slack, PagerDuty or email.
