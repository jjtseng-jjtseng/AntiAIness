What Pulse records

Three series per endpoint, plus the timeline that puts them next to your deploys. Each series is kept for your plan's retention window.

Latency
p50, p95 and p99 for every endpoint. The table above shows p95 because that is the number most teams alert on; p50 and p99 are recorded alongside it.
Error rate
The share of responses that failed, per endpoint. The sample above shows a service running at 0.42% overall, with a 5xx spike on one endpoint.
Throughput
Requests per minute, per endpoint, so a latency change can be read next to the load it happened under rather than in isolation.
Incident timeline with deploy markers
Every deploy is a marker on the timeline, so an incident and the release before it sit on the same line, which is how #4821 ends up named on the chart above.

Alerts with per-endpoint thresholds

A p95 ceiling that is right for /v1/search is wrong for /v1/checkout. Pulse sets thresholds per endpoint, on latency, error rate or throughput, and routes each rule to the place the on-call engineer will see it.

Each rule picks its own destination: Slack, PagerDuty or email.

Where alerts go

  • SlackFor the rules the whole team should see as they fire.
  • PagerDutyFor the endpoints that should wake someone up.
  • EmailFor the rules that can wait until morning.
