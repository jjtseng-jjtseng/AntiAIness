What we build

Three kinds of job. Most projects are the first one; the other two are for people who already have a website and need it to work harder.

  • A new website

    For a business with nothing yet, or only a social media page.

    Usually four to seven pages: home, one page per service, about, contact, and whatever your customers ask about most. We help with the writing, choose the photos with you, and set up the domain and hosting in your name.

    Includes
    Page plan, design, build, mobile layout, contact form, a walkthrough of how to update it.

  • A redesign of the site you have

    For a site that is out of date, hard to update, or embarrassing on a phone.

    We start with what is already there: which pages people actually visit, what is wrong, what makes you wince. Then a new structure and look. Old web addresses are redirected so nothing you have printed or shared stops working.

    Includes
    Review of the current site, new page plan, design, build, redirects, content moved across.

  • One piece, done properly

    For a site that mostly works but is missing something.

    A contact form that reaches you, a menu or price list you can change yourself, a gallery of your work, a booking link, an accessibility fix. Small jobs on a site we did not build are fine.

    Includes
    The piece itself, tested on phones, with a short note on how to maintain it.

Not sure which of these you need? Describe the problem in the brief form and we will tell you.

How a project goes

Four stages, in this order. You see a written plan and a price before any design starts, and you see the site as it comes together.

Start a project brief

  1. A conversation

    What you do, who calls you, what a good year looks like, and what the website has to do to help with that.

    You bring: nothing. We ask the questions.

  2. A page plan and a quote, in writing

    The pages, what each one is for, and the features you need. Priced before any design work, so there is a number to say yes or no to.

    You get: a one-page plan like the one at the top of this page.

  3. Design and build

    You get a private link that updates as the site is built, so you can open it on your own phone and send it to whoever you want a second opinion from.

    You give: feedback, photos, and the words only you know.

  4. Launch and handover

    The site goes live on your domain. We walk through how to change the text, photos and prices yourself, and leave you written notes.

    You keep: the domain, the hosting account and the files.

Phones first

Every layout is designed at phone width before desktop, because that is where most first visits to a local business come from.

Plain English

Emails and calls in ordinary words. If a technical term matters, we explain it once and write it down.

Nothing held hostage

Domain, hosting and files are registered to you. If you ever move on, you take everything with you.

Questions people ask before starting

If yours is not here, put it in the brief form and we will answer it directly.

What kind of websites does CodeCraft build?

Websites for small businesses, independent makers, service providers and community projects: usually between one and ten pages, with a contact form, a service or product list, photos of real work, and a way to update it yourself. We do not build online shops with hundreds of products or custom web applications.

Can you redesign a website I already have?

Yes. We start by looking at what you have: which pages get visited, what is out of date, and what is hard to change. Then we plan the new structure, move your content across, and redirect the old addresses so links you have shared keep working.

How much does a website cost?

It depends on the number of pages, the features, and how much of the writing and photography you already have. After the first conversation you get a written quote for a fixed scope, before any design starts. Hosting and the domain are paid by you, in your name, so there is no ongoing fee to us.

Will my website work on mobile?

Yes. We design the phone layout first and test on real phones, not only in a desktop browser. Phone numbers are tap-to-call, forms work with autofill, and text stays readable without zooming.
