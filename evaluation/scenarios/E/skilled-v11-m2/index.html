How a project runs

Four stages. Each one ends with something in your hands to check, so nothing about the finished site comes as a surprise.

Talk through your idea
  1. 1

    We listen

    A conversation about what you do, who your customers are, and what a good result would look like to you.

    You get a short written summary of what we heard, so you can correct anything we got wrong.

  2. 2

    We plan the pages

    Every page listed with its job, what goes on it, and the features it needs: forms, galleries, menus.

    You get the page plan, like the example at the top of this page. Design starts once you’ve agreed it.

  3. 3

    We design and build

    Design and development happen together in the browser, so you react to the real site rather than a picture of one.

    You get a private link to the work in progress, to open on your own phone and comment on.

  4. 4

    We launch

    We check every page on phones, tablets and desktops, fix what turns up, and put the site live.

    You get notes on what’s where and what to do next.

In every site, whatever the size

A one-page site gets the same care as a twelve-page one.

  • Laid out for phones firstThen widened for tablets and desktops, and checked on each before launch.
  • Readable by everyoneComfortable text sizes, strong contrast, and every link and button usable from a keyboard.
  • One clear next step per pageCall, book, visit or donate: each page ends with the thing you want visitors to do.
  • Plain words throughoutFrom the first chat to the handover, we explain our choices without jargon.

Good to know before we start

Every project is a little different. If your question isn’t here, put it in the brief below.

What kind of websites does CodeCraft build?

Custom websites for small businesses, independent makers, service providers and community projects: usually a handful of pages that explain what you do, show your work and make it easy to get in touch. The right scope depends on what your site needs to do.

Can you redesign a website I already have?

Yes. We start by going through your current site with you: what people use, what they skip, and what’s out of date. Good content usually stays. The structure and the visual direction are what change.

How much does a website cost?

It depends on three things: how many pages you need, any features beyond the basics (a booking link, a menu, a gallery), and whether your words and photos are ready. We work out the scope together in the page plan, so you know exactly what’s included before anything is built.

Will my website work on mobile?

Yes. Every page is laid out for a phone first and then widened for larger screens, and we check it on real phones before launch.

What do I need to have ready?

Not much. A sense of what you do and who you want to reach is enough to start. If you have a logo, photos of your work or an existing site, bring those along too.

Tell us what you’re building

Answer five questions and we’ll set your answers out as a one-page project brief. Read it back, copy it, or change anything that doesn’t sound right.

What makes a useful brief

  • Say what you do in one sentence, the way you’d say it to a customer.
  • Name the one thing you want visitors to do: call, book, visit or donate.
  • Mention a website you like, even one from a different line of work.
